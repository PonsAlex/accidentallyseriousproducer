Genuine Soundware - MIDI-AXE

MIDI work is where a session loses an hour: something is not receiving, the routing is wrong, the sysex will not load, and the machine that should be making a decision is you, at the wrong end of a cable. Ohlhorst Digital - Examer

Examer is not a plugin that makes sound, it is an application that finds out what other things actually do. It measures plugins and outboard gear in stereo, rendering offline with sample-exact test signals, which is the only way to compare two pieces of gear without the comparison lying to you. That makes it the tool you reach for when a plugin claims to model something and you want to know whether it does. It is free through Ohlhorst's newsletter, runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, and pairs with the TDR Collector if you already use it.

Verdict: Uma bancada de medição para plugins e hardware, com sinais exatos. Grátis com a newsletter.

Riverflux Audio - Spatial EQ

Every EQ gives you frequency, gain and Q. Spatial EQ adds a fourth per band — depth — which is the one control that decides whether a boost brings a sound closer or moves it away. That is the difference between making something louder in a range and making it feel nearer, and it is a distinction mixers usually chase with reverb, delay and a lot of guessing. It is free on Gumroad, which is where a lot of the small experimental tools live now.

Verdict: Um EQ com uma quarta direção: profundidade. O mesmo boost pode trazer um som para frente ou empurrá-lo para trás.

Sonescent - Imager

Most stereo tools show you width. Imager shows you loss — what disappears when the mix is folded to mono, which is how it will be heard on a phone, a club system and a smart speaker. That reframes the tool: instead of widening until it sounds impressive on headphones, you widen until nothing important vanishes on a single speaker. It is free, needs an email rather than a card, carries no licence key, and ships as VST3, AU and AAX with free updates.

Verdict: Um imager construído em volta da pergunta que os outros evitam: o que essa mixagem perde em mono.

Audiorift - DUEL LT

DUEL LT is the free half of one idea, and the idea is the reason to care: stop stacking a compressor, a saturator and a limiter, and put the three in a single box where each feeds the next. A Charger to glue dense instrument groups, a Rift to inject even-order harmonics, a Shield to hold the peaks. Three fixed stages, one result, and no decisions about which order they go in. DUEL XT, the paid version at $48.51, opens the same engine: six saturation modes instead of one, three limiter characters instead of a brickwall, plus calibrated EQ, intelligent gain matching and control over gain and the final transient. The difference is not better versus worse, it is speed versus control. The LT gives you the result in one move; the XT lets you decide how the result is built, which matters when the last two per cent of a sound is carrying half of its meaning. For a free plugin the LT is unusually opinionated, and that is the compliment.

Verdict: Um motor de dinâmica em três estágios para tirar uma trilha de dentro de uma parede de orquestra. Grátis, v1.1.3, AAX/AU/VST3.

Steinberg - Classics Collection

Steinberg invented VST in 1996, and this collection is the archaeology: Neon, one of the first polyphonic software synths; VB-1, a bass guitar; CS-40, a subtractive synth from the earliest VST era; LM-7, a sample-based drum unit; and Karlette, a tape delay. Verdict: The plug-ins that started VST in 1996, re-released together: Neon, VB-1, CS-40, LM-7 and Karlette.

13x Audio - LOFT

Most reverbs sit still: you send a sound in and a space comes back. LOFT moves the tail along a curve — five built in, or one you draw yourself — measured in seconds from each new sound or in fractions of a bar, so the decay lands on the grid instead of wherever physics put it. On top of that sits a key engine with 72 tuned resonators, so the tail rings in the song's key rather than against it, and FREEZE holds the whole thing where you left it. Three converters from 1986, 1989 and 1992 do the colour. It is free for macOS, Windows and Linux — VST3 everywhere, plus AU on the Mac — and the page says the two words that matter: no account, no sign-up.

Verdict: A reverb whose tail keeps time and stays in key, and free with no account. VST3 on macOS, Windows and Linux.
