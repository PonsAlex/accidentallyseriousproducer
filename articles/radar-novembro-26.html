Pyro is a cinematic starter kit: 269 effects across eleven categories — braams, impacts, sub drops, risers, whooshes, alarms, glitches, interface sounds, ambience — delivered as 24-bit WAV up to 192 kHz, 3.5 GB, royalty-free. Verdict: 269 cinematic effects in eleven categories, 24-bit WAV, free until the end of November for an email address. The radar's first sample pack.

Busy Works Beats - Soft Clipper 3000

Soft Clipper 3000 does the two jobs beatmakers keep reaching for: catch the peaks without squashing the life out of them, and show you what the gain is actually doing while you do it. Busy Works Beats gives it away for an email address. It runs as AU and VST3 on macOS 11 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel, and as VST3 on 64-bit Windows.

Verdict: A soft clipper and limiter with precision metering. The metering is the reason to keep it; the clipping is a crowded field.

FFOSSO

FFOSSO is a place to start rather than a plugin you reach for: a modular grid that combines pre-mapped samples with sequencer, chord and layer engines, built to get a session moving before the blank page wins. The free tier carries 11 instruments, over 100 multi-sampler presets and 14 integrated devices; Pro adds more than 150 instruments and weekly content for $10 a month. The installer offers three parts — a standalone application of about 623 MB, which is mandatory, plus optional VST3 and AAX builds of about 14 MB each — so it works on its own or inside the DAW, and the whole thing needs a little over 640 MB of disk. Windows only.

Verdict: A real free tier, a background process and more than 600 MB of downloads. The idea is good; the risk is that it becomes furniture.

RiverFlux Audio - BIT LAYER

Most bit crushers give you one knob and a wet/dry. BIT LAYER gives you eight independent layers, each with its own bit depth, sample rate, jitter, effect order, mid/side routing and gain — and a frequency target, so the destruction lands on the bass while the hats stay clean. Tonal and transient modes split sustained sounds from sharp attacks, and there are 88 presets if the routing matrix looks like too much on a Tuesday. It comes from RiverFlux Audio on Gumroad at a price you name, zero included. VST3 and CLAP on Windows and Linux, plus AU on macOS 12 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel.

Verdict: Eight crush layers with their own frequency targets and gain, and it runs on Linux. The most distinctive thing from this house.

RiverFlux Audio - Relic

Relic is not one plugin but fifteen, each installed on its own: five compressors, five equalisers, two tape machines, a channel strip and two reverbs, complete with meters, needles and the rest of the theatre. Fifteen separate units is a strange way to ship a suite and a good way to use one — you load the Fairchild-alike on the drum bus and nothing else comes with it. The store shows over eleven hundred ratings, which for a free bundle at a price you name is a lot of people who bothered to say something. Zero is an accepted price.

Verdict: Fifteen classic studio units, each installed as its own plugin, free at a price you name. Eleven hundred people bothered to rate it.

RiverFlux Audio - ECHO LAYER

A single delay applied to a whole sound makes one decision for everything in it. ECHO LAYER splits the signal into four delay layers and gives each its own frequency target and its own tonal or transient behaviour — so the low end can have a short slap while the tail of the reverb keeps ringing. It is the same philosophy as BIT LAYER from the same house: separate the parts, then treat each one. Zero is an accepted price.

Verdict: Four delay layers with frequency targeting. Good, and the third delay from a house that already took two spaces.

RiverFlux Audio - PHIXER

Wide bass sounds impressive in the room and disappears in mono. PHIXER exists for that moment: it fixes phase problems on a stereo bass while leaving the width you were enjoying, with EQ, low and high cut, wet/dry and mid/side controls around a single large phase dial. It is the least glamorous plugin in the shop and probably the one that saves the most mixes. Zero is an accepted price.

Verdict: Phase correction for stereo bass. No glamour at all, and it saves mixes.

Puremagnetik - Studies

Studies is Puremagnetik's plugin library, split into Studies Effects for audio tracks and Studies Instruments for MIDI, and ten of its devices are free in full — every control, every preset, and the page says plainly that they do not expire. The names tell you where they land: Auras shapes ambient texture, Driftmaker disintegrates delay, Olden doubles tape with a wear control you can follow, Phanes reflects spectral grain, The Barber slices harmonic animation, Verv bakes tape loops in the sun. Each device is drawn as its own signal flow, so the knob you turn is sitting on the stage it controls — a small idea that makes the whole library legible. The rest of it is a $9/month subscription; these ten are not a trial of it.

Verdict: Ten devices in full, every control and preset, and the page says they do not expire. The subscription is for the rest of the library, not for these.

NOLVRA - GLOAM

GLOAM watches the spectrum, finds the frequencies that stand above their neighbours and ring, and pulls each one down only while it rings — so the rest of the take keeps its level instead of being dulled along with the problem. That is the job soothe2 made expensive, and NOLVRA says so on the page: same class, not a copy, and they do not claim it sounds the same. The terms are the unusual part. No account, no licence key, no time limit, nothing on the face locked behind a paid tier — a tone generator and a resonance suppressor given away outright. VST3 on Windows 10 or later; VST3 and AU on Apple Silicon, macOS 11 or later.

Verdict: A free resonance suppressor that does soothe2's job. No account, no key, no time limit — and the vendor says plainly it is not a copy.

OXO-Music - Oxo Synth2

Synth1 was the free synth half the internet learned on, and Daichi Laboratory stopped maintaining it. Mac users kept it alive through Rosetta, which goes away in macOS 28. Oxo Synth2 is the answer to that: a faithful recreation with the same parameters and the same UI, 100% compatible with existing Synth1 patches and banks, plus MIDI Learn and themes. The synth engine was deliberately left alone. It is free and open source, so the next abandonment cannot kill it. Today it runs on macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel, as VST3 and AU, and as an AUv3 on iPadOS 17; the Windows VST3 is listed as in progress with no date.

Verdict: Synth1 rebuilt in the open, patch-compatible, engine untouched. macOS today; the Windows build is listed as in progress.

DUY Audio - DaD Vinyl

DaD Vinyl does not layer a noise sample over your track; it models the record and the playback chain, from 33 and 45 RPM vinyl to 78 RPM shellac. Rumble, disc damage, tone and stereo image are separate controls, pops and scratches go as far as you like, and there is turntable dust and hiss on top — plus a year setting, so the character matches the era you are after. Subtle warmth or a much-played disc are both on the dial. It normally costs $56.35 and is free for two weeks. The catch is in the format list: AU, VST, VST3 and AAX for macOS 12 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel, and no Windows version at all. No iLok dongle either, and the installers are 14 to 24 MB.

Verdict: A .35 vinyl and shellac model, free until 20 November. Mac only, and the listing still says Beta.

Knightsnare gives away five plugins, all at zero and none of them demos. KNIGHT8000 is a drum machine built from scratch on the 808 — every sound synthesised, no samples. BDR-1000 is Bogren Digital's tuner, listed at €19 and currently at zero. DJW Audio - DW-303

DW-303 is a TB-303-style bass synth with the parts that matter: a step sequencer, slides and accents, which is the whole reason the 303 still turns up in records. It is free and open source under GPL-3.0-or-later, at version 0.1.0, with no licence key and no activation — you download it and it works. The format list is unusually generous: VST3, AU, AAX, CLAP, LV2 and a standalone build on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs; VST3, CLAP and standalone on Windows 10 and 11; and VST3, CLAP, LV2 and standalone on Linux, which almost nothing in this catalogue bothers to support. Two honest warnings are on the page itself: the Windows installer is not yet signed, so SmartScreen will complain, and the Linux build wants GTK 3 and WebKitGTK 4.1.

Verdict: An open-source 303 with slides and accents, no key, no activation, and it runs on Linux. Version 0.1.0, so stability is the question.

Goeran Meyer - MYR ZENITH

ZENITH puts a whole mastering chain in a single window, in the order a mastering engineer would build it: utility, corrective EQ, harshness control, opto leveller, multiband, low end, glue, brilliance, saturation, imager, limiter. Each module switches, the EQ does mid/side with cuts up to 96 dB per octave, and the limiter is true-peak with soft clip, auto release and dither. The panel measures what the streaming services will do with the result — integrated LUFS, LRA, true peak, PSR and PLR — and LEARN sets the limiter so the target is actually met rather than approximated. It ships with 28 presets, an eight-page manual in English and German, and a price line that is only two words long: free, no account, no licence key. The page adds a sentence most vendors avoid: it is free and stays free.

Verdict: A twelve-module mastering chain with LEARN and streaming-accurate metering. Free, no account, no key — and the page says it stays free.
